# Still Out There — Interactive Storybook Storyline

Nov 10, 2026 · @darling

## Concept

The reader becomes a rookie **Signal Keeper** at a giant radio dish on Earth. One night a faint signal arrives from Voyager 1, the farthest machine humans ever built. To answer it, the Keeper must first "wake the memories" of the explorers that came before.

**The hook is real:** on November 18, 2026, Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth. A radio message takes 24 hours to reach it. The story ends at that moment.

**Narrator:** Voyager 1 speaks to the reader in short, warm messages between chapters. Each artifact also tells its own chapter in first person.

**Game theme:** a retro mission-control game. Each world is a level on a star map. Reading pages and finishing mini-games charges **Signal Energy**, which unlocks the next level. Every artifact you wake gives a **Mission Patch** and an **Explorer Card**.

**Core message:** these machines are not lost or dead. Some still send data, some are still used by scientists, and all of them changed what we know.

**Title:** *Still Out There*

## Pacing for all ages

Every page has three reading layers, so a 6-year-old and a 60-year-old read the same page at their own depth.

| Layer | Who it's for | Length | How it shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spark | Ages 5+, skimmers | 1 line, max 12 words | Big text, always visible |
| Story | Ages 8+, most readers | 2–4 short sentences | Below the Spark, always visible |
| Deep Dive | Teens and adults | 3–6 facts with numbers | Tap "Mission Log" to open |

**Chapter rhythm.** Every level follows the same six beats, so readers always know what comes next:

1. **Arrival:** a wide, calm image of the world.
2. **Wake-up:** the artifact introduces itself.
3. **Challenge:** a 30–90 second mini-game based on its real engineering.
4. **Discovery:** what the science found.
5. **Last day:** a quiet, honest ending.
6. **Reward:** patch, card, and a short Voyager message.

**Pacing rules**

- Never more than 3 reading pages before an interaction.
- Alternate loud beats (landing, launch) with quiet beats (silence, sunset).
- Each level takes 5–8 minutes. The whole book takes about 35–45 minutes.
- Progress saves after every page, so readers can stop anytime.
- Every page can be read aloud with captions.

## Storyline

Seven parts: a prologue, five levels moving outward from Earth, and a finale. Sample Spark and Story lines are written for the key pages; Deep Dive facts are in each level's Mission Log.

### Prologue — The Faint Signal

**Setting:** a radio dish on Earth at night.

- **Spark:** "Beep. Something very, very far away is calling."
- **Story:** Your first night as a Signal Keeper. The screen flickers with a weak message from Voyager 1. It's farther from home than anything humans have ever made, and it has a request.
- **Voyager's message:** "Hello, Keeper. I am almost one light-day from you. Before you answer me, visit my friends. They went first. Wake their memories, and you will understand why I'm still out here."
- **Interaction:** tune the dish by turning a dial until the static clears. This is the tutorial.

### Level 1 — The Moon (1967–1972)

**Wake-ups:** Surveyor 3 → Apollo 11 laser reflector → Apollo seismometer → Apollo 15 rover.

- **Surveyor 3:** "I landed in 1967 to see if the ground was safe. Two years later, astronauts walked over to visit me!" Apollo 12 landed about 180 m away, and the crew cut off parts, including its camera, to bring home.
- **The reflector (the star of this level):** "I'm just a panel of special mirrors. But I'm still working."
  - **Challenge:** fire a laser from Earth and watch it bounce back about 2.5 seconds later.
  - **Discovery:** timing that bounce shows the Moon moves about 3.8 cm farther from Earth every year, about as fast as your fingernails grow.
- **The seismometer:** "I felt the Moon shake." It discovered moonquakes and was switched off in 1977.
- **The rover:** "I'm a car parked on the Moon. My keys? There aren't any." Three Lunar Roving Vehicles are still parked there.
- **Quiet beat:** Earth rising over the grey horizon, no music.

### Level 2 — Mars (1976–2024)

The longest level and the emotional heart of the book. Each robot is a generation, and each one helps the next.

- **Viking 1 (1976):** "I sent back the first picture ever taken from the surface of Mars. I tested the soil for life." It worked until 1982.
- **Sojourner (1997):** "I was the first rover on Mars, no bigger than a microwave. I landed bouncing inside giant airbags."
  - **Challenge:** the airbag bounce landing.
  - Built for about a week, it worked for almost three months.
- **Opportunity (2004–2018):** "They gave me 90 days. I stayed almost 15 years."
  - **Challenge:** drive and clean dusty solar panels when a dust devil passes. Real winds did clean Opportunity's panels.
  - **Discovery:** tiny round rocks nicknamed "blueberries" showed that water once soaked the ground.
  - **Last day:** a huge dust storm in 2018 blocked the Sun. Its last signal came on June 10, 2018. Engineers sent over 1,000 commands, hoping it would wake. It never did.
- **InSight (2018–2022):** "I listened to the heartbeat of Mars."
  - **Challenge:** spot a marsquake on a wiggly seismograph.
  - **Discovery:** over 1,300 marsquakes, which helped measure Mars's core.
- **Ingenuity (2021–2024):** "I was a test. Five flights, they said. I flew 72." It made the first powered flight on another planet.
- **Voyager's message:** "You see? Each one was braver because of the one before."

### Level 3 — Rock and Fire (2001–2015)

A short, fast-paced level after the emotional Mars chapter.

- **NEAR Shoemaker:** "Nobody built me to land. But in 2001, I landed on an asteroid anyway."
  - **Challenge:** a gentle landing on asteroid Eros, at walking speed.
  - It was the first spacecraft to orbit an asteroid and land on one.
- **MESSENGER:** "I circled Mercury, the planet closest to the Sun, and found ice hiding in its shadowy craters." In 2015, out of fuel, it crashed into Mercury as planned.

### Level 4 — The Outer Dark (1972–today)

- **Pioneer 10:** "I was the first to fly through the asteroid belt and past Jupiter. I carry a picture of you." Its plaque shows humans and where Earth is. The last faint signal came in 2003.
- **New Horizons:** "I traveled more than nine years to meet Pluto. It had a heart!" It flew past Pluto in 2015, then visited Arrokoth in 2019.
  - **Challenge:** snap a photo during a high-speed flyby. You get one chance.

### Finale — One Light-Day

- **Voyager 1 tells its own story:** launch in 1977, Jupiter, Saturn, and the Pale Blue Dot photo of Earth taken in 1990.
- **The Golden Record:** the reader opens a playable gallery of the record's sounds and pictures (descriptions only, no copyrighted songs reproduced).
- **Crossing:** in 2012, Voyager 1 became the first spacecraft in interstellar space.
- **The honest part:** its power is low. To keep it alive, engineers have turned off instrument after instrument. Only two science instruments are still on.
- **Final interaction:** the reader writes a short message and presses Send. A timer shows the real 24-hour, one-way journey (sped up on screen).
- **Final Spark:** "They're quiet now. But they're still out there. And so is the next explorer: you."

## Game mechanics

Every mini-game teaches one real piece of engineering, and none of them can be failed. A miss just gives a hint and another try, so young readers never get stuck.

| Level | Mini-game | Real engineering it teaches | Time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue | Tune the dish | Radio antennas pick up faint signals | 30 s |
| Moon | Laser bounce | Light travel time measures distance | 45 s |
| Mars | Airbag landing | Sojourner's airbag landing system | 60 s |
| Mars | Dust devil cleanup | Solar power and Mars weather | 60 s |
| Mars | Find the marsquake | Seismometers read wave patterns | 45 s |
| Rock and Fire | Gentle touchdown | Slow, careful landing in low gravity | 60 s |
| Outer Dark | One-shot flyby photo | Flybys happen fast, with no second chance | 45 s |
| Finale | Send a message | Radio signals take time to cross space | 90 s |

**Signal Energy.** Each page read adds 1 bar and each mini-game adds 5 bars. A level unlocks once the bar is full. Reading the Deep Dive gives bonus energy, which rewards curious readers without punishing skimmers.

**Collectibles**

- **Mission Patches:** one per level, shown on the Keeper's jacket.
- **Explorer Cards:** one per artifact, with launch year, status badge, and "superpower."
- **Status badges:** "Still talking" (Voyager 1 and 2, New Horizons), "Still useful" (laser reflectors), and "Resting" (everything else).

**Difficulty modes:** *Cadet* for young readers, with bigger targets and hints, and *Commander* for older ones, with real numbers on screen. Same story, same ending.

## Zustand state map

Keep the story as static data and keep only the reader's progress in Zustand, saved with `persist` so a reader can close the tab and continue later.

**Story data (static, not in the store)**

```ts
type Layered = { spark: string; story: string; deepDive?: string[] };

type Page = Layered & {
  id: string;                 // "mars-opportunity-3"
  artifactId?: string;        // links to an Explorer Card
  beat: 'arrival' | 'wakeup' | 'challenge' | 'discovery' | 'lastDay' | 'reward';
  miniGame?: 'tuneDish' | 'laserBounce' | 'airbag' | 'dustDevil' | 'marsquake' | 'touchdown' | 'flybyPhoto' | 'sendMessage';
  audioSrc?: string;
};

type Level = { id: string; title: string; energyToUnlock: number; pages: Page[] };
```

**Store**

```ts
import { create } from 'zustand';
import { persist } from 'zustand/middleware';

type Status = 'talking' | 'useful' | 'resting';

interface StoryState {
  levelId: string;
  pageIndex: number;
  completedLevels: string[];
  signalEnergy: number;
  patches: string[];
  cards: string[];                     // artifactIds collected
  finishedGames: string[];
  difficulty: 'cadet' | 'commander';
  settings: { narration: boolean; captions: boolean; textSize: 1 | 2 | 3; reducedMotion: boolean; highContrast: boolean };
  playerMessage: string;               // finale message to Voyager

  nextPage: () => void;
  prevPage: () => void;
  addEnergy: (n: number) => void;
  finishGame: (gameId: string) => void;
  collectCard: (artifactId: string) => void;
  completeLevel: (levelId: string, nextLevelId: string) => void;
  setSetting: <K extends keyof StoryState['settings']>(k: K, v: StoryState['settings'][K]) => void;
  reset: () => void;
}

const initial = {
  levelId: 'prologue', pageIndex: 0, completedLevels: [], signalEnergy: 0,
  patches: [], cards: [], finishedGames: [], difficulty: 'cadet' as const,
  settings: { narration: false, captions: true, textSize: 2 as const, reducedMotion: false, highContrast: false },
  playerMessage: '',
};

export const useStory = create<StoryState>()(
  persist(
    (set, get) => ({
      ...initial,
      nextPage: () => set((s) => ({ pageIndex: s.pageIndex + 1, signalEnergy: s.signalEnergy + 1 })),
      prevPage: () => set((s) => ({ pageIndex: Math.max(0, s.pageIndex - 1) })),
      addEnergy: (n) => set((s) => ({ signalEnergy: s.signalEnergy + n })),
      finishGame: (id) => {
        if (get().finishedGames.includes(id)) return;   // no double rewards
        set((s) => ({ finishedGames: [...s.finishedGames, id], signalEnergy: s.signalEnergy + 5 }));
      },
      collectCard: (id) => set((s) => (s.cards.includes(id) ? s : { cards: [...s.cards, id] })),
      completeLevel: (levelId, nextLevelId) => set((s) => ({
        completedLevels: [...new Set([...s.completedLevels, levelId])],
        patches: [...new Set([...s.patches, levelId])],
        levelId: nextLevelId, pageIndex: 0,
      })),
      setSetting: (k, v) => set((s) => ({ settings: { ...s.settings, [k]: v } })),
      reset: () => set(initial),
    }),
    { name: 'still-out-there-save' }
  )
);
```

**Tips:** select small slices in components, like `useStory((s) => s.signalEnergy)`, so pages don't re-render on every change. Derive "is level unlocked" from `signalEnergy >= level.energyToUnlock` instead of storing it.

## Accuracy checklist

Voyager status was checked against current reporting. The other mission facts are approximate and should be checked against each NASA mission page before the demo.

- [ ] **Don't use the famous "My battery is low and it's getting dark" line as Opportunity's real words.** It was a reporter's poetic summary, not a message the rover sent. Opportunity only sent data.
- [ ] Voyager instrument count: re-check close to the demo, since NASA keeps turning instruments off to save power.
- [ ] The one light-day date (November 18, 2026) is from NASA's Voyager page.
- [ ] Golden Record: describe its contents; don't play or print copyrighted music or lyrics.
- [ ] Every artifact speaking in first person should carry a small note: "Story told from the explorer's point of view. Facts are real."
- [ ] Use NASA images, which are generally free to use with credit, and follow NASA's media guidelines.
