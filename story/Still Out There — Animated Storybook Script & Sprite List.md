# Still Out There — Animated Storybook Script & Sprite List

Nov 13, 2026 · @darling

An animated, hand-drawn crayon storybook in 7 parts and 44 spreads. Every fact comes from the verified storyline; the sprite list at the end is everything to generate.

## How the book works

The reader turns pages of a living picture book. Each **spread** is one animated scene with text, and some spreads have a **tap moment** that makes the story happen. Nothing can be failed.

**Every spread has:**

- **Scene:** what the picture shows.
- **Animation:** what moves on its own.
- **Tap:** an optional moment the reader triggers.
- **Spark:** one big line, max 12 words, for young readers.
- **Story:** 2–4 short sentences for most readers.
- **Mission Log:** extra facts behind a tap, for older readers.

**The frame story.** Kit, a new Signal Keeper, and their robot friend Echo pick up a faint message from Voyager 1. Voyager asks them to visit the explorers that came before it. The radio signal works like a door into each explorer's memory, so Kit and Echo appear in each scene as glowing visitors in little space helmets.

**Style bible**

- Hand-drawn children's storybook look: wax crayon and gouache, visible strokes, paper grain, slightly wobbly outlines, soft painted shadows.
- Warm, cozy palette even in space. Each part has an accent color: Prologue night blue, Moon silver-blue, Mars 1 rust orange, Mars 2 dusty pink, Small Worlds violet, Beyond deep teal, Finale gold.
- **NASA hardware keeps its real shape.** It is drawn softly and cutely, but it never gets a face. An explorer "speaks" through a gentle glow pulse and a speech panel. This keeps the book accurate.
- Characters move at 12 frames per second with a light **line boil**, the hand-drawn wobble that makes drawings feel alive. The boil stops in reduced-motion mode.
- UI is torn paper cards with crayon borders and a handwritten title font.

**Main characters**

- **Kit, the Signal Keeper:** about 10 years old, short curly hair, round glasses, big yellow headphones, an oversized teal jacket covered in mission patches, orange sneakers. Curious and brave, a little nervous at first.
- **Echo:** a small round robot about knee-high to Kit. Its body is like a soft tin can with a dark screen face and two big round friendly eyes. Its head is a small white radio dish with one antenna and a glowing tip. It has stubby arms and rolls on one wheel. Echo never speaks words; it beeps and its antenna glows.
- **Voyager 1:** drawn accurately with its big white dish and long booms. Its messages appear in a glowing "incoming signal" panel.

## Prologue: The Faint Signal

Three spreads. A calm, starry opening that ends with Voyager's request.

**P.1 · First night on the job**

- **Scene:** a grassy hilltop at night. A giant radio dish points at the stars. Kit stands below it with a flashlight; Echo rolls beside them.
- **Animation:** stars twinkle, the dish turns slowly, Echo's antenna blinks once, then faster.
- **Spark:** "It's your first night as a Signal Keeper."
- **Story:** "The big dish listens to the sky. Most nights it hears only a soft hiss. Tonight, Echo's antenna starts to blink."

**P.2 · Someone is calling**

- **Scene:** inside the cozy control hut. A round crayon screen shows wild, fuzzy static.
- **Animation:** the static wiggles; Kit leans in.
- **Tap:** turn the big dial until the static smooths into a clear wave. Echo spins happily.
- **Spark:** "Beep… beep… Someone is calling."
- **Story:** "The signal is faint, but it's real. It comes from Voyager 1, the farthest machine people have ever built."

**P.3 · Hello, Keeper**

- **Scene:** deep space. Voyager 1 glides past, with the Sun as a tiny bright star behind it.
- **Animation:** rings of signal ripple from Voyager's dish toward a faraway Earth.
- **Spark:** "Hello, Keeper."
- **Story (Voyager):** "I am almost one light-day from home. Before you answer me, visit my friends. They went first. Wake their memories, and you'll understand why I'm still out here."
- **Note:** after November 18, 2026 this becomes "more than one light-day from home."
- **Transition:** the doodle-pattern chapter screen, with Echo rolling across.

## Chapter 1: The Moon — "The Ones Who Proved It Was Possible"

Nine spreads, 1967–1977. The robots test the ground, then people land, then the science stays behind.

**1.1 · Welcome to the Moon**

- **Scene:** a gray, dusty plain under a black sky. Earth hangs blue and white above the horizon. Kit and Echo appear in a shimmer of signal light, wearing little bubble helmets.
- **Animation:** dust drifts up slowly with each of Kit's steps.
- **Spark:** "Welcome to the Moon. No air. No sound. Lots of dust."
- **Story:** "Long before people walked here, robots came first. One of them is standing right over there."

**1.2 · Surveyor 3 bounces in**

- **Scene:** a memory flashback. Surveyor 3 drops toward the ground on its three legs.
- **Animation:** the engine keeps firing; the lander hops, hops again, then settles.
- **Tap:** press "Engine off!" to start the landing and watch it bounce.
- **Spark:** "I bounced twice before I settled."
- **Story (Surveyor 3):** "My job was to check whether the Moon's ground could hold a spaceship. My engine didn't shut off in time, so I hopped across the dust."
- **Mission Log:** landed April 20, 1967. Bounced to about 10 m, then 3 m, then settled.

**1.3 · A little scoop**

- **Scene:** close-up of Surveyor's scoop arm over the soil.
- **Tap:** drag the scoop to dig a trench.
- **Spark:** "A little scoop. A big answer."
- **Story (Surveyor 3):** "I dug trenches and pushed on the soil. It felt like wet sand, strong enough to hold a lunar module. People could come!"
- **Mission Log:** sent 6,326 TV pictures. Last contact May 4, 1967.

**1.4 · The Eagle's bottom half**

- **Scene:** the Apollo 11 lunar module, gold foil shining, on four legs in the Sea of Tranquility.
- **Animation:** the top half lifts off in a spray of sparkles; the bottom half stays behind.
- **Spark:** "Half of me flew home. Half stayed as a launch pad."
- **Story (Eagle):** "In July 1969, two astronauts landed in me. When they left, my top half blasted off from my back. I've stood here ever since."

**1.5 · A mirror that still works**

- **Scene:** the laser reflector, a small panel of mirrors, sitting on the gray ground. Earth shines above.
- **Tap:** fire a laser from Earth. The beam travels to the Moon, bounces, and comes back about 2.5 seconds later. A counter ticks.
- **Spark:** "I'm a mirror that scientists still use."
- **Story (reflector):** "People on Earth still shine lasers at my mirrors and time the bounce. That's how they know the Moon drifts about 3.8 cm farther away every year, as fast as your fingernails grow."
- **Badge:** Still useful.

**1.6 · Visitors for Surveyor**

- **Scene:** Apollo 12 has landed. Two astronaut silhouettes walk toward the old, dusty Surveyor 3.
- **Animation:** the astronauts wave; Surveyor glows warmly.
- **Spark:** "Two years later, I had visitors!"
- **Story (Surveyor 3):** "In 1969, Apollo 12 landed about 180 meters from me. The astronauts took pieces of me home, so scientists could see how I survived on the Moon."

**1.7 · Making the Moon ring**

- **Scene:** the Apollo 12 science station: a central box, a seismometer, and a finned nuclear battery, connected by cables.
- **Tap:** drop the empty ascent stage far away. A seismograph line wiggles… and keeps wiggling.
- **Spark:** "Listen… the Moon is ringing."
- **Story (science station):** "The crew crashed their empty ascent stage into the Moon on purpose, and I felt it shake. It was the first recorded artificial moonquake."

**1.8 · A car parked on the Moon**

- **Scene:** the Apollo 15 Lunar Roving Vehicle near tall, rounded mountains.
- **Animation:** the rover unfolds, then drives off, kicking up dust and leaving tracks.
- **Spark:** "I'm a car parked on the Moon. Forever."
- **Story (rover):** "I rode folded up inside the lander. On Apollo 15, the astronauts drove me to explore far from their landing site. Together they covered 27.9 km."

**1.9 · The quiet Moon** (quiet page)

- **Scene:** a wide view: lander base, rover, mirrors and footprints, all still. Earth over the horizon. Echo sits down, a little sad.
- **Animation:** only the slow twinkle of stars. No music.
- **Spark:** "The Moon is quiet now. But we're still here."
- **Story:** "On September 30, 1977, the science stations were switched off. Footprints, wheels and mirrors still wait under the black sky."
- **Voyager:** "They proved we could land. Now watch where that led."

## Chapter 2: Mars — "The First Visitors"

Seven spreads, 1976–2008. The first explorers to land, roll and dig on Mars.

**2.1 · The red planet**

- **Scene:** a rocky orange plain under a butterscotch sky. Kit and Echo shimmer in.
- **Animation:** a light breeze pushes dust along the ground.
- **Spark:** "Mars is red, dusty, and very cold."
- **Story:** "Reaching Mars takes months. And landing is the hardest part of all."

**2.2 · The first picture**

- **Scene:** the Viking 1 lander on its three legs, its camera turned toward its own footpad.
- **Animation:** a crayon "photo" builds up one stripe at a time, just like Viking's camera scanned it.
- **Spark:** "I took the first picture ever on Mars."
- **Story (Viking 1):** "I landed on July 20, 1976. Minutes later, I sent home a picture of the rocky ground beside my foot."

**2.3 · Is anything alive?**

- **Scene:** Viking's long arm reaching for the soil.
- **Tap:** scoop soil and drop it into Viking's tiny onboard lab. Bubbles and sparkles puzzle everyone.
- **Spark:** "Is anything alive in here?"
- **Story (Viking 1):** "Some of my tests looked a bit like life. Another test found no carbon-based chemicals at all. Scientists still argue about what it meant."
- **Mission Log:** sent weather reports for years. A wrong command from Earth cut contact in November 1982.

**2.4 · The bouncy ball landing**

- **Scene:** sky view. A giant cluster of airbags falls toward a rocky valley.
- **Tap:** release the airbags, then watch them bounce about 15 times and roll to a stop.
- **Spark:** "I landed inside a giant bouncy ball!"
- **Story (Sojourner):** "In July 1997, my lander fell wrapped in huge airbags. We bounced as high as 15 meters before rolling to a stop."

**2.5 · The first rover**

- **Scene:** the Pathfinder lander opens like a flower. Little six-wheeled Sojourner waits on its ramp.
- **Animation:** the airbags shrink, the petals open, and Sojourner rolls down onto Mars.
- **Spark:** "I'm only 30 cm tall, but I was first!"
- **Story (Sojourner):** "I was the first rover on Mars. I drove up to rocks to study them. Rounded pebbles showed that water once rushed through this valley."
- **Mission Log:** the lander is now called the Carl Sagan Memorial Station. The last data came on September 27, 1997.

**2.6 · Now you see it…**

- **Scene:** a flat, frosty plain near the north pole, cracked into big polygon shapes. The Phoenix lander with its two round solar panels.
- **Tap:** dig a trench with Phoenix's arm. White bits appear. Then a time-lapse runs "Day 1… Day 4" as the white bits vanish.
- **Spark:** "Now you see it… now you don't!"
- **Story (Phoenix):** "I landed near the north pole on May 25, 2008. I dug a trench and found white bits. Four days later they were gone. They were ice, turning straight into vapor in the thin, dry air!"

**2.7 · Winter comes** (quiet page)

- **Scene:** the same plain at dusk. Frost creeps over Phoenix's panels. Echo pulls its arms in, cold.
- **Animation:** snow-like frost slowly gathers. No music.
- **Spark:** "Winter came, and the Sun went away."
- **Story (Phoenix):** "I was built for one summer. In November 2008, the long winter came and my solar panels stopped, just as planned."
- **Voyager:** "They were the first visitors. Each one made the next one possible."

## Chapter 3: Mars — "The Long Drivers"

Nine spreads, 2004–2026. The emotional heart of the book: twins, a listener, a flyer, a goodbye, and a record finally passed.

**3.1 · The twins**

- **Scene:** a split page. On the left, Spirit in a crater; on the right, Opportunity on a flat plain. Mars curves between them.
- **Animation:** both rovers raise their camera masts and look around at the same moment.
- **Spark:** "We were twins, sent to opposite sides of Mars."
- **Story:** "Spirit landed on January 3, 2004, and Opportunity three weeks later. They were built for about 90 days. Their mission: find clues about water from long ago."

**3.2 · Catch the wind**

- **Scene:** Opportunity on a dusty plain. Its solar panels are covered in a layer of dust. A swirling dust devil dances in the distance.
- **Tap:** steer Opportunity under the passing dust devil. The dust blows off and the panels sparkle.
- **Spark:** "Dusty panels? Catch the wind!"
- **Story (Opportunity):** "Dust slowly covered my solar panels, and my power dropped. But sometimes Martian winds swept them clean, and I woke up with fresh energy."

**3.3 · The rocks remember**

- **Scene:** a close-up of layered rock dotted with tiny round pebbles.
- **Animation:** a soft blue "memory wash" shows water lapping over the rock long ago, then fades.
- **Spark:** "The rocks remembered water."
- **Story:** "The twins found rocks that could only form where water stayed for a long time. Long ago, Mars was a wetter world."

**3.4 · Stuck in the sand**

- **Scene:** Spirit with its wheels sunk in soft, pale sand.
- **Animation:** the wheels spin, sand puffs up, Spirit settles.
- **Spark:** "Spirit's wheels got stuck."
- **Story:** "After driving 7.7 km, Spirit sank into soft sand. It kept studying the ground right where it stood, until its mission ended in 2010."

**3.5 · The listener**

- **Scene:** InSight on a flat, smooth plain with two round solar panels. Its arm sets a dome-covered seismometer on the ground.
- **Tap:** listen to the seismograph line and tap when a marsquake wiggle appears.
- **Spark:** "I listened to the heartbeat of Mars."
- **Story (InSight):** "I landed in November 2018. My robot arm placed a super-sensitive seismometer on the ground. Then I listened, very carefully."

**3.6 · What's inside Mars**

- **Scene:** Mars sliced open like a fruit, showing its crust, mantle and core.
- **Animation:** quake waves ripple through the layers and light each one up.
- **Spark:** "Quakes showed what hides inside Mars."
- **Story (InSight):** "Marsquake waves traveled through the planet and showed scientists its crust, mantle and core. Slowly, dust covered my panels. My mission ended in December 2022."

**3.7 · Five flights? Seventy-two!**

- **Scene:** Jezero Crater. The tiny Ingenuity helicopter with its two stacked rotors, and the Perseverance rover watching nearby.
- **Tap:** hold to lift off, hover in the glowing band, then land on the flat spot.
- **Spark:** "They said five flights. I flew seventy-two."
- **Story (Ingenuity):** "I rode to Mars under Perseverance's belly. On April 19, 2021, I made the first powered flight on another planet, in air less than 1% as thick as Earth's."
- **Mission Log:** flew 17 km in total and reached 24 m high. A rotor was damaged on its last flight on January 18, 2024. It now stands still on Mars.

**3.8 · The storm** (quiet page)

- **Scene:** Opportunity alone on a wide plain. The sky turns from butterscotch to a thick brown haze as a planet-wide dust storm rolls in.
- **Animation:** the haze thickens very slowly. Echo moves close to Kit. No music.
- **Spark:** "Opportunity, the other twin, kept driving. Then came the storm."
- **Story:** "For almost 15 years, Opportunity rolled on, 45.16 km in all. In 2018, a dust storm wrapped the whole planet. Its last signal came on June 10, 2018."

**3.9 · The record**

- **Scene:** the Perseverance rover rolls past a small crayon marker post labeled "45.16 km."
- **Animation:** confetti-like sparkles burst softly; Echo cheers.
- **Spark:** "A new rover passes the old record."
- **Story:** "On October 11, 2026, Perseverance drove farther than Opportunity ever had. The old record was finally broken."
- **Voyager:** "Records are made to be passed. That's how explorers say thank you."

## Chapter 4: Small Worlds — "Small Worlds, Big Firsts"

Six spreads, 2000–2018. A quick, playful chapter after Mars. It is called "Small Worlds" rather than "The Asteroid Belt" because Eros, where NEAR landed, travels near Earth's orbit, while Vesta and Ceres sit in the main belt between Mars and Jupiter.

**4.1 · Lumpy little worlds**

- **Scene:** a starry space scene with tumbling rocky shapes. A long, peanut-shaped asteroid, Eros, turns slowly in the foreground.
- **Animation:** the rocks spin gently at different speeds.
- **Spark:** "Out here, the worlds are small and lumpy."
- **Story:** "Some small worlds wander near Earth. Others crowd together in a belt between Mars and Jupiter. Two brave explorers visited them."

**4.2 · The surprise landing**

- **Scene:** the NEAR Shoemaker spacecraft (an eight-sided body with four solar panels and a dish) floating above Eros' cratered surface.
- **Tap:** give gentle little pushes to slow NEAR down and touch down softly. A speed meter shows walking speed.
- **Spark:** "Nobody built me to land. I landed anyway."
- **Story (NEAR):** "I was the first spacecraft to orbit an asteroid. At the end of my mission, on February 12, 2001, my team lowered me gently onto Eros, about as fast as you walk."

**4.3 · Still talking**

- **Scene:** NEAR resting on the dusty surface, one solar panel tipped up toward the Sun.
- **Animation:** tiny signal rings pulse from NEAR's dish, then slow and stop.
- **Spark:** "Still talking, from the surface of an asteroid!"
- **Story (NEAR):** "I wasn't built to survive landing, but I did, and I kept sending data for about two weeks. In all, I took 160,000 pictures. Then the deep cold, about −173 °C, quieted me."
- **Mission Log:** last contact February 28, 2001. Renamed in honor of geologist Eugene Shoemaker.

**4.4 · The gentle engine**

- **Scene:** the Dawn spacecraft with its very long solar-panel wings and a soft blue glow from its ion engine.
- **Tap:** hold a gentle, steady push for a while. A dotted path slowly curls from Vesta toward Ceres.
- **Spark:** "I visited two worlds and stayed with both."
- **Story (Dawn):** "My ion engine gave the gentlest push, like a breath. But it kept pushing for weeks at a time. That let me orbit the giant asteroid Vesta, then fly on to the dwarf planet Ceres."
- **Mission Log:** orbited Vesta 2011–2012 and arrived at Ceres in early 2015. The first mission to orbit two bodies besides Earth and the Moon.

**4.5 · The bright spots**

- **Scene:** dark gray Ceres with glittering white spots inside a large crater (Occator).
- **Animation:** the camera zooms slowly toward the crater; the spots sparkle.
- **Spark:** "What were those bright spots?"
- **Story (Dawn):** "Ceres had shining spots inside a crater. They turned out to be salt, left behind when salty ice from below turned to vapor."

**4.6 · Still circling** (quiet page)

- **Scene:** Dawn as a small silhouette circling Ceres, its wings catching faint sunlight.
- **Animation:** one slow orbit. No music.
- **Spark:** "Out of fuel, but still circling."
- **Story (Dawn):** "In 2018, I ran out of the fuel I used to point myself. I still circle Ceres today, quietly."
- **Voyager:** "Small worlds. Big firsts. Keep going, Keeper."

## Chapter 5: Beyond — "Eyes on the Stars"

Six spreads, 1972–today. A telescope that found other worlds, and two probes carrying a picture of us outward.

**5.1 · Watching stars blink**

- **Scene:** the Kepler telescope (a tube with a slanted sunshade and a solar panel) drifting in space, staring at a rich star field. Earth is a small blue dot ahead of it.
- **Tap:** watch one star's brightness line and tap each time it dips. Each dip reveals a tiny planet passing in front of the star.
- **Spark:** "I found planets by watching stars dim."
- **Story (Kepler):** "I launched in 2009 and stared at one patch of sky. When a star dimmed just a tiny bit, it meant a planet was passing in front of it."

**5.2 · The kayak trick**

- **Scene:** Kepler wobbling, with crayon arrows of sunlight pushing on it. A small kayak doodle in the corner shows the idea.
- **Animation:** Kepler tilts, then the sunlight arrows balance it until it holds steady.
- **Spark:** "A broken wheel. A clever fix."
- **Story (Kepler):** "In 2013, I lost one of the wheels that kept me pointing steady. Engineers used the gentle push of sunlight to balance me, like a kayak steering into the current. My second life was called K2."

**5.3 · More planets than stars**

- **Scene:** the night sky filling up: around each star, a tiny planet appears.
- **Animation:** planets pop in one by one until the whole sky is full.
- **Spark:** "More planets than stars!"
- **Story (Kepler):** "I found more than 2,600 planets and showed that there are more planets than stars in our sky. I ran out of fuel in 2018. Now I drift around the Sun, trailing behind Earth."

**5.4 · A picture of you**

- **Scene:** Pioneer 10 (a big dish on a small body, with long thin booms) racing past giant, stripy Jupiter. A small gold plaque glints on its side.
- **Tap:** tap each part of the plaque to reveal what it means: the people, the Sun, the planets, the spacecraft.
- **Spark:** "I carry a picture of you to the stars."
- **Story (Pioneer 10):** "I launched in 1972. I was the first spacecraft to cross the asteroid belt and fly past Jupiter. On my side is a plaque showing people and where Earth is."

**5.5 · My twin went to Saturn**

- **Scene:** Pioneer 11 swinging past Saturn and its rings.
- **Animation:** Saturn's rings shimmer as the probe crosses.
- **Spark:** "My twin flew to Saturn first."
- **Story:** "Pioneer 11 was the first spacecraft to fly past Saturn. It carries the same plaque, the same picture of us."

**5.6 · Two ghost ships** (quiet page)

- **Scene:** two tiny probes drifting apart into a vast, dark star field. A faint red star (Aldebaran) glows far ahead of one.
- **Animation:** the probes move almost too slowly to notice. No music.
- **Spark:** "Quiet now, and very far away."
- **Story:** "Pioneer 11 last spoke in 1995. Pioneer 10's last faint signal came on January 23, 2003, from 12.23 billion km away. In about two million years, it will pass a red star called Aldebaran."
- **Voyager:** "They carried your picture. Now, Keeper, it's time for my story."

## Finale: One Light-Day

Four spreads. Voyager tells its own story, the reader answers, and every explorer lights up.

**F.1 · My own story**

- **Scene:** a montage page in three panels: a rocket launch, Voyager passing stripy Jupiter, then Voyager passing ringed Saturn.
- **Animation:** the panels slide in one after another, like a flip book.
- **Spark:** "Now, my own story."
- **Story (Voyager):** "I launched in 1977. I flew past Jupiter and Saturn. In 2012, I became the first spacecraft in interstellar space."

**F.2 · The honest part** (quiet page)

- **Scene:** Voyager alone in deep space, its glow soft and low. Small instrument lights on its body, most of them off.
- **Animation:** one tiny light dims; the others stay off. A slow, steady signal pulse continues.
- **Spark:** "I'll be honest with you, Keeper."
- **Story (Voyager):** "My power is low. Only a couple of my instruments are still on. But I'm still out here, still sending my signal home."
- **Check before the demo:** the instrument count can change.

**F.3 · Answer me**

- **Scene:** back on the hilltop at night. Kit types at a little console under the giant dish. Echo watches, antenna glowing.
- **Tap:** write a short message (up to 140 characters) and press Send. A glowing pulse leaves the dish and travels along a timeline labeled "24 hours to Voyager," sped up on screen.
- **Spark:** "Now you can answer me."
- **Story (Voyager, date-aware):** "On November 18, 2026, I reach one light-day from Earth, about 25.9 billion km. Your message will take a whole day to reach me. Send me one, Keeper."

**F.4 · Still out there**

- **Scene:** Kit and Echo sitting on the hill under a sky full of stars. One by one, small glowing markers appear across the sky for every explorer they met.
- **Animation:** the markers light up in story order; Echo leans against Kit.
- **Spark:** "They're quiet now. But they're still out there."
- **Story:** "And so is the next explorer: you."
- **Then:** the Explorer Cards collection and the Credits.

## Sprite list: how to generate

Generate still images only. The code adds all the motion (wobble, bobbing, parallax, camera moves), so you never need frame-by-frame animation.

**Order of work**

1. **Make the style reference first.** Generate one test image (Kit standing) until the crayon look feels right. That image becomes the style reference for everything else.
2. **Character sheets next.** Generate Kit and Echo, then use them as reference images in every later prompt so they stay the same.
3. **Hardware with NASA photos.** Upload a NASA photo of each spacecraft as a reference so its real shape is kept. Ask for the crayon style on top.
4. **Backgrounds last**, using the style reference.

**Style prompt (put at the start of every prompt)**

```text
Hand-drawn children's storybook illustration, wax crayon and gouache texture, visible crayon strokes, soft paper grain, slightly wobbly outlines, gentle painted shadows, warm cozy muted palette, friendly and calm, no text, no letters, no logos, no flags.
```

**Sprite prompt ending (characters, hardware, props)**

```text
[SUBJECT, POSE], full body, centered, isolated on a plain solid white background, even lighting, no shadow on the background.
```

**Background prompt ending**

```text
Wide 16:9 landscape scene, [DESCRIPTION], no characters, no spacecraft, keep the lower middle area open and simple for characters to stand in, important details in the center third.
```

**Rules that save time later**

- **Plain white backgrounds for sprites.** Claude Code will cut them out automatically with a background-removal tool, so you don't need transparent PNGs.
- **No faces on NASA hardware.** Real shape, soft crayon look, nothing added.
- **No text in images.** AI tools write garbled letters; all text is added by the code.
- **Backgrounds wide (16:9, 2K).** The app crops them for phones, so keep the important parts in the center.
- **Name files exactly as in the lists** (for example `kit_idle.png`) and put them in one folder. Claude Code will wire them up by name.
- **Budget:** Higgsfield's Plus plan lists 1,200 credits as roughly 600 Nano Banana Pro images, so about 2 credits per image ([source](https://recurdash.com/subscription-pricing/higgsfield)). The lists below have about 70 must-have and 15 nice-to-have images. With retries, plan for about 130–150 images (260–300 credits), so generate all the **Must** items first.
